Website Design in Kenya

Straight answers to the questions business owners actually ask before starting a website project.

Everything below, in one folder, before the kickoff call. Missing items are the number one cause of project delays — not the design work itself.

  • Business name & tagline
  • Logo files (PNG, SVG, AI/PDF)
  • Brand colors & fonts (if defined)
  • High-res product/service photos
  • Written text for every page
  • Contact details (phone, email, WhatsApp)
  • Physical address & Google Maps pin
  • Social media links
  • List of services or products
  • Prices (if shown publicly)
  • About-us story & team bios
  • Certifications, awards, partners
  • Domain name (or approval to buy one)
  • Hosting account or approval to set up
  • Existing site login (if any)
  • Google Analytics / Search Console access
  • Google Business Profile access
  • M-Pesa Paybill/Till number (if selling)
  • Sample sites you like (and why)
  • Sample sites you dislike (and why)
  • Target customer description
  • Main competitor sites
  • Key pages you want (e.g. Home, About, Contact)
  • Primary call-to-action (call, buy, book, sign up)
  • Any legal text (privacy policy, terms)
  • Single decision-maker for approvals
  • Your deadline & launch date
  • Budget range, confirmed in writing
  • Preferred communication channel & hours
  • Login details for any tools they'll need

If you don't have some of these yet — like professional photos or written copy — tell the team upfront so they can quote for it or adjust the timeline.

Expect anywhere from KES 15,000 to over KES 80,000 depending on what you need. A simple 3–5 page starter site usually sits around KES 15,000–25,000. A proper business site with lead capture, local SEO, and mobile-first design falls in the KES 35,000–60,000 range. Ecommerce with M-Pesa integration starts around KES 80,000 and goes up.

Don't forget the yearly costs: domain registration (roughly KES 1,000–2,000/year) and hosting (KES 2,500–9,000/year) are separate from the build fee.

A well-built site gives you the foundation to rank — clean structure, fast loading, mobile-friendly pages, and proper titles. But ranking for competitive terms takes time and usually content beyond your homepage.

The practical minimum: make sure your site loads in under 5 seconds on mobile, includes your location and service keywords naturally, and is linked to a Google Business Profile. That alone puts you ahead of many Kenyan small business sites that are still desktop-only and slow.

Simple sites: 3–7 days. Standard business sites with a few pages and basic SEO: 2–4 weeks. Custom builds or ecommerce with many products: 4–8 weeks or more.

The biggest delay is usually not the developer — it's waiting for content (text, photos, logo files, approvals). Have your materials ready before you start and you'll move much faster.

Yes — if you ask for it upfront. Many developers build static sites where every text change requires their help (and a fee). If you want to change prices, add services, or post updates yourself, request a CMS (content management system) or a blog dashboard during the quote stage.

Expect to pay a small upgrade fee (often KES 5,000–10,000 one-time) and a slightly higher yearly renewal for the CMS. Worth it if you plan to keep the site current.

You should — but confirm it in writing. The domain name should be registered under your business email or your own account, not the developer's personal account. At handover, you should receive admin access to the hosting, the domain registrar, and any analytics.

For the design and content: ask explicitly whether you get full ownership or a license to use the site. Some developers retain rights to the underlying code or theme. A clear contract avoids problems later.

It must. The vast majority of Kenyan web traffic comes from phones, and Google ranks based on the mobile version of your site. A desktop-only design is a direct loss of customers.

What to check: pages load in under 5 seconds on a normal 4G connection, buttons are large enough to tap easily, menus work with one thumb, and contact forms don't have too many fields. If a developer can't show you a mobile preview before launch, walk away.

Yes, M-Pesa integration is standard for Kenyan ecommerce. You'll need a Paybill or Till number linked to a Safaricom Daraja account. Most WordPress/WooCommerce sites can add M-Pesa through a plugin, or a developer can build a custom checkout flow.

Setup takes a few days to get the Daraja credentials approved. Factor in transaction fees from Safaricom and make sure the checkout shows a clear "Pay with M-Pesa" button on mobile.

Budget roughly KES 4,000–12,000 per year for a typical small business site. That covers domain renewal (KES 1,000–2,000) and hosting (KES 2,500–9,000). If you have a CMS, add a bit more for the higher-tier hosting.

Maintenance (software updates, security checks, small content changes) is often separate — either a monthly retainer or pay-per-edit. Ask upfront what's included in the hosting fee vs. what costs extra.

Most developers include one or two revision rounds. That means after you see the first draft, you send one consolidated list of changes (colors, layout, text tweaks). After that, further changes may be billed hourly or per request.

To get the most out of your revision: gather all feedback from your team into a single document, be specific ("change the header background to #1e293b" not "make it darker"), and decide who has final approval before sending anything.

Ask for a written scope and a live example of their mobile work. A clear scope lists exactly what pages you get, what features are included, how many revisions, who owns what at the end, and the total cost with no surprises.

The mobile example matters because a beautiful desktop portfolio means nothing if the sites break on phones. Open their example site on your own phone, test the menu and contact form, and see how fast it loads on your network. That tells you more than any sales pitch.
